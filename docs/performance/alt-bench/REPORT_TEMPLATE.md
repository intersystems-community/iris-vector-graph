## Verdict

- **Parity is reached, within the measurement noise of this machine, with named exceptions.** Each suite is taken from its newest run
  (`final-1` to `final-8` and `quiet-1` to `quiet-5`, 3 to 12 interleaved repetitions, container restarted before each pass, side-B tree
  checked identical to the committed branch, namespaces tuned the same way): {{LATEST_COUNTS}}. The nine slower rows: the
  betweenness-dispatch test duration (1.16, overlapping repetitions), three stress-ingest rows (a 1,000-edge `bulk_create_edges` call
  whose cost is a fixed two seconds of index disable, rebuild and sync per call on both sides, and two node tests timed at 10 ms
  resolution), two bidirectional-PPR timing tests (the baseline's stored counters double across the test's two fixtures; the tests time
  10 to 40 ms), the networkx control row of the head-to-head (1.11; that row runs no IVG code), `cypher-parser-bench` q4 (a 0.5
  microsecond difference that disappears with the garbage collector off) and one p95 of `unit-cypher-benchmark` (1.17, five repetitions
  that overlap). Each is explained in the parity section. A slower row that rests on repetitions overlapping A's is noise at this
  resolution; none is a regression I could reproduce in a tight loop.
- **The one gap that looked real was stale statistics.** `quiet-1` showed the medium graph's `Q1` at 0.64 ms on A and 1.02 ms on B. Both
  sides ran the same SQL in separate namespaces; the optimizer plans from table statistics, and a namespace loaded after its last
  `TUNE TABLE` planned the same statement 40 times slower (20 s against 0.5 s on an SNB query). After the harness runs
  `GraphSchema.tune_tables` right after every dataset load (protocol rule 14), the medium `Q1` is 0.95 ms and the large one 0.69 ms
  (`quiet-3`, `quiet-4`): level. `quiet-5` re-ran spec-193 (51 of 51 metrics at parity).
- **An audit after the first version of this report found that some parity rows were parity of empty or failing answers** (shortest
  path pairs that were the seed, a weighted shortest-path CALL that searched for nodes named "a" and "b", a benchmark whose data
  lacked the property its queries filter on, long traversals on the 100,000-node graph failing on both sides). They are listed in
  their own section. The harness now reads answer sizes and engine errors; `bench.py` Q5 and Q6 and `unit-cypher-benchmark` were
  fixed and re-run (`final-5`, `final-7`: every leg real, all level). The 100,000-node graph's long traversals (`ivg-os` Q2 to Q4, `ivg-arno` Q3 and Q4)
  failed on both sides with the string-size error; a chunked fallback that fires only when the string call overflows now returns their
  full answers (34,702, 44,965 and 45,333 nodes) and the baseline tree gets the same methods at export (`final-8`, `quiet-3`: every leg
  real). Still not real work: the three `e2e-gates-analytics` tests that assert a non-empty result and fail on both sides. Four translator defects the audit and the LDBC queries exposed (present in the
  baseline, absent from the TCK) are fixed on the branch with tests.
- **The slowdowns measured before the fixes were real.** The first benchmark pass had `KHopCount` 270x to 18,000x slower
  because every degree was recounted on every read; the generation-validated cache, the whole-graph warm stamp and the single
  writer module fixed that. Three further regressions found while benchmarking are listed in the next section with their fixes.
- **Against Neo4j and Memgraph the picture is mixed and depends on the query shape** (caveats below before comparing):
  - On the R-MAT graphs Neo4j and Memgraph answer the count-style variable-length queries (Q2 to Q4, spec-193 `[*1..N]`) faster
    than IVG at every size, because IVG's `BFSFastJson` entry points ship every edge (M Q4: IVG 129 ms, Neo4j 12.7 ms). Memgraph
    falls far behind IVG as depth and size grow (M Q4: 5.2 s against IVG's 129 ms).
  - Weighted shortest path (Q6): IVG is faster than both at S (0.5 ms against 3.3 and 9.7) and slower than Neo4j at M (18 ms
    against 4.5; Memgraph 143 ms). On the like-for-like 10,000-row LDBC KNOWS graph IVG is faster than both on IC13 and faster than
    Neo4j on IC3 (Memgraph is faster on IC3, IC2 and the friend count).
  - Algorithms at 2,000 nodes: IVG's betweenness (70 ms) is the default 200-source approximation (`sample_size=0`), not an exact
    run, so it is not comparable with the exact timings it was set beside (GDS 239 ms, igraph 199 ms, networkx 5.4 s, Memgraph's `nxalg`
    12.8 s, Memgraph MAGE's native procedure 26 ms) and I withdraw the earlier reading of it as faster; the exact comparison is in the
    5,000 to 20,000-node section; Leiden (140 ms) beats GDS (772) and igraph (363); GDS is
    faster on closeness (27 ms against 175) and on exact betweenness at 500 nodes; the in-process references are far faster on
    degree.
  - Six more BI-style aggregates at SF0.1: Neo4j and Memgraph 4 to 25 times faster. Exact betweenness at 5,000, 10,000 and 20,000
    nodes: IVG 30, 140 and 610 s against GDS 2.25, 3.75 and 15.9 s (13 to 39 times slower); harmonic closeness 5.4, 21.7 and 86.5 s
    against 0.12, 0.20 and 0.71 s (45 to 120 times slower). The sides are level on all of them.
  - Interactive updates on the SNB graph (eight inserts and the eight deletes that undo them): 190 ms to 1.7 s per update on IVG without
    a `(key, value)` index on `rdf_props`, 0.7 to 5.3 ms with one, against 5.6 to 7.9 ms on Neo4j and 0.3 ms on Memgraph.
  - Full LDBC SNB schema at SF0.1 and the 535M-edge temporal graph are in their own sections below: multi-join property queries
    are roughly 10 to 900 times slower on IVG than on Neo4j and Memgraph, and loading is 10 to 17 times slower through the engine's own paths (25 to 45 times in the first measurement, which loaded edges through a silent per-row fallback; see the SNB section).

## Machine, sides and method

- One macOS host; IRIS 2026.3.0AI.113 enterprise in `ivg-iris-enterprise` (one container, two namespaces, `IVGBENCHA` and
  `IVGBENCHB`); Neo4j 5.24.2 Community with GDS 2.12.0, Memgraph 3.9.0, networkx 3.7, python-igraph 1.0.0 in other containers
  or a venv on the same Docker VM (8 CPUs, 64 GB). Competitors ran one at a time, never alongside an IRIS run.
- **Side A** is the 4.2.3 release (`6f17be0f`) with its own Python tree; **side B** is branch `alt-parity` (the alternative:
  one writer module for the adjacency, a generation-validated degree cache, no stored `deg`/`degp`). A and B were run
  interleaved (A, B, A, B, ...) so drift hits both. Per-run commits and load averages are in each run's `manifest.json`.
- Background load on this host was a load average of 8 to 14 during the timed runs (security agents, the macOS window server, OrbStack);
  two unrelated debug processes that were using about 1.7 cores were stopped first. Drift between sessions on the same side: the Q2 to Q4 medians stay
  within 5 % between passes, while Q1 (a 0.5 ms query) moved 9 % to 22 % on both sides alike. Treat differences inside roughly
  10 % as not distinguishable from drift.
- Verdict rule (`PROTOCOL.md`): parity when B/A is at most 1.10 or B's median lies inside A's spread; faster below 0.90;
  otherwise slower. A sub-microsecond metric is judged by absolute difference (0.25 microseconds); that rule was added after
  seeing the first measurement and is disclosed there.

## What happened on the way (read this before trusting any number)

1. **Run `core-2` is not used.** Partway through it IRIS stopped responding: the Write Daemon stalled and IRIS paused all
   users, after hours of multi-gigabyte loads on a container that had been up for days. I restarted the container. After the
   restart the same suite ran in 33 s instead of about 220 s, so everything taken before the freeze in that run was degraded.
   Its five completed suites were re-run (`core-4`); `core-2` stays in the repository, labelled, and is excluded from every
   count below. `core-1` ran earlier and before the translator fix; it is kept as the "before" picture.
2. **Three regressions were found and fixed during the run**, none of them the cache design:
   - 4.2.4's parameter-isolation walk in the Cypher translator took 80 % of `translate_to_sql` (0.03 ms to 0.15 ms on a
     short query). It is now cached on the cached syntax tree (no clash: one attribute read) and copy-on-write when a name
     really clashes.
   - A bulk load left the degree cache empty, so the first read after a load paid a recount per node (`perf-head-to-head`:
     load 1.58x, degree 2.3x). The bulk-load session now warms the cache once before the two-hop statistics.
   - Bulk readers paid three global reads per node where the stored counter paid one; a whole-graph "warm" stamp (valid
     while no write has bumped `^KG("__version")` since the cache was filled) brought that to one.
3. **Baseline A accumulates drift that the benchmarks do not clean up.** The bidirectional-PPR performance tests deleted
   rows by SQL and left `^KG` behind, so on side A each repetition walked a different, smaller graph (the result size varied
   between runs: 995, 989, 909, 492 nodes) and looked faster; side B always returned the same answer. From a clean state both
   sides return identical results and first-call times within about 10 %.
4. **A claim in ADR-0004 does not hold in the engine.** The engine's Native API handle is a dedicated connection, so a SQL
   `rollback()` does not undo adjacency written through it. A caller who disables autocommit and rolls back by SQL leaves
   adjacency behind, on the baseline as well. The rollbacks the engine guarantees are the ones ObjectScript owns.

## Did the parity rows measure real answers? (an audit added after the first version of this report)

A parity ratio is only worth something if the query returned an answer. The harness parsed latencies only, and `execute_cypher`
returns an empty result with `.error` set when the database rejects a statement, so a failing query is timed as a fast zero-row
success. Three read-only audits (the earlier runs, the TCK, the translator) and a classifier over the raw `bench.py` output
(`scripts/bench/alt/validity.py`) found:

- **Real work**, same answer on both sides: `bench.py` Q1 to Q4 on S and M, the spec-193 hop queries on S, the stress suites
  (traversal, search), the head-to-head algorithms (Pearson 1.0 against networkx), the KGBENCH queries, and the migration, FHIR
  and DRKG runs.
- **Parity of an empty or failing answer**, not of work: `bench.py` Q5 on every size (the shortest-path pair was always
  `node_0 -> node_0`, a zero-hop answer) and Q6 (weighted shortest path, zero rows on both sides); on the 100,000-node graph
  `ivg-os` Q2 to Q4 and `ivg-arno` Q3 and Q4 failed every call on both sides (`MAXSTRING`: the edge list outgrows IRIS's string
  limit) and their latencies are the time to fail; `unit-cypher-benchmark` returned zero rows on every call; and two analytics
  gates (`test_returns_nonempty_results`) fail on both sides.
- In the `final-1` table below, S Q5 shows one row and is classed "real"; that row is the zero-hop path from the seed to itself.
- The tables below classify each `bench.py` leg. Q5 now uses a pair that is not the seed (`bench_utils.pick_shortest_path_pair`
  excludes the seed), and Q6 had a second cause: `CALL ivg.shortestPath.weighted($a, $b, ...)` read its `$parameters` as the text "a" and
  "b" (the handler never received the query's parameters; fixed on the branch with tests, and `bench.py` now inlines the ids so both
  sides run the same call and raises on an empty or failed answer). `final-5` re-ran both on S and M: every leg is real and level.
  On the 100,000-node graph the long traversals failed on both sides with the string-size error (`<MAXSTRING>`, or `<STRINGSTACK>` inside the
  engine); the chunked fallback fixed that and the pair finder, so Q2 to Q6 all return answers there (`final-8`, `quiet-3`).

### Why the TCK did not catch the translator defects found along the way

The TCK harness (vendored openCypher TCK, 3,896 eligible scenarios, run strict and typed against a live IRIS) does treat an engine
error as a failure, so a swallowed SQL error would fail a scenario. The defects below sit outside what it contains: it has no
`$parameter` inside a node property map (none of its features uses `{k: $p}`), no aggregate alias in `ORDER BY` combined with
`LIMIT` outside `WITH`, and no `shortestPath` scenario at all; its graphs are tiny, and it is not part of CI.

| defect (all present in the 4.2.3 baseline)                                                                                                                                          | effect                                                                | status                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a `$parameter` in a node property map next to an undirected hop bound the AST text, not the value                                                                                   | `MATCH (p:Person {sid: $id})-[:KNOWS]-(f)` matched nothing, silently  | fixed on `alt-parity` with a test                                                                                           |
| an aggregate alias in `ORDER BY` with `LIMIT` was re-emitted outside its derived table                                                                                              | SQLCODE -23 at prepare, empty result                                  | fixed with tests                                                                                                            |
| `shortestPath` with labelled or property-map endpoints took its endpoints from the wrong place                                                                                      | zero rows (or a constant `hops = 1`)                                  | fixed with tests; `execute_cypher(raise_on_error=True)` added                                                               |
| a string `$parameter` compared with `<`, `>` was cast to DOUBLE                                                                                                                     | ISO dates compared as their year                                      | fixed with tests                                                                                                            |
| `n.prop >= $x` and `<=` repeated the operand (the NaN-safe form) but bound the parameter once                                                                                       | `Incorrect number of parameters` at execution; no rows                | fixed with tests (`test_comparison_parameter_binding.py`); the benchmark trees predate it, so BI02 and BI16 use `>` and `<` |
| `MATCH (a:Person), (b:Person) WHERE a.sid = $a … CREATE (a)-[:R]->(b)` read `$a`, `$b` as node ids when the parameter names equal the variables                                     | foreign-key failure                                                   | open on the baseline; the alternative resolves them                                                                         |
| `engine.betweenness_centrality` caps at 200 sources whatever `sample_size` says; table `DELETE` leaves `^KG`; a fresh engine uses the per-row load path until `initialize_schema()` | approximate results labelled exact; stale adjacency; 10x slower loads | open (product; same on both sides)                                                                                          |
| the baseline's stored degree counters do not follow a SQL delete                                                                                                                    | a rebuilt graph carries doubled degrees; PageRank answers differ      | removed by the alternative (counters derived)                                                                               |
| `-[:KNOWS*1..3]-` builds every trail as one JSON string in `CY_VLP_PATHS`                                                                                                           | `MAXSTRING` above two hops on a social graph                          | open                                                                                                                        |
| strings that look like numbers are ordered numerically by `CY_SORT_KEY`                                                                                                             | ties and `LIMIT` cut differently from Neo4j and Memgraph              | open                                                                                                                        |
| no `(key, value)` index on `rdf_props`; no `TUNE TABLE` after a bulk load                                                                                                           | the same SQL ran 40x apart in two identical namespaces                | rule added to the protocol                                                                                                  |

## Breadth beyond the core suites

### Upgrade from 3.x to 4.0.0 (`scripts/bench_migration_4_0_0.py`: 50,000 nodes, 50,000 vectors of 128 dimensions)

{{MIGRATION_TABLE}}

Six interleaved reps with a 20 s settle gap between runs. This was the largest regression found: the first runs
(`migration-1` to `migration-3`) had the alternative at 2.4x to 3.7x the baseline's apply time. The cause was a chain of three
things, each fixed: a shortcut read path in the node-store migration, classes that failed to compile before the graph-scoped
schema existed (leaving a half-compiled class state), and doubled round trips in the backfill. One effect remains: when the two
class versions are deployed alternately into the same scratch namespace in quick succession, an `ALTER TABLE` can wait 10 to
120 s for a lock the other version still holds. That is an artefact of alternating the benchmark, not a cost of upgrading, and
the settle gap removes it. The faster `step.embeddings` and `profile.rekey` rows are plausibly the one-statement backfill used
when a single graph exists; I did not isolate that.

### The full LDBC SNB schema (Spark datagen 0.5.1 `csv/raw/composite-merged-fk`; SF0.1 and SF1)

All eight entity types and fifteen relationship types of the interactive workload, loaded into IVG (baseline and alternative, each
in its own namespace), Neo4j and Memgraph. SF0.1 is 448,684 nodes and 2,164,444 edges; SF1 is 3,727,429 nodes, 21,140,212 edges and
25,003,650 property rows. Sixteen read queries (short reads, complex reads, three aggregates) run with the same parameters on every
engine (start people drawn with a fixed seed from the better-connected half; Neo4j and Memgraph ran 20 parameter sets per query, IVG
20 for the quick queries and as few as 1 for the slowest, the same sets on both IVG sides, so the slowest rows rest on one or two
timings); `answers` says whether the engines returned the same rows, by an order-insensitive digest. Both IVG sides' tables were
tuned (`TUNE TABLE`) the same way before anything was timed.

**SF0.1** was loaded into IVG by the engine's own bulk node and `bulk_load_session` edge paths, into Neo4j and Memgraph by batched
`UNWIND`. IVG appears twice. The first load used a freshly constructed engine: `capabilities.objectscript_deployed` stays `False`
until `initialize_schema()` runs, so `bulk_ingest_edges` silently took its per-row Python path (1.5 thousand edges per second). The
second load calls `initialize_schema()` after the nodes and before the edges, which selects the server-side loader (10 to 11
thousand edges per second); nodes are loaded before that call on purpose, because the server-side node loader writes every property
through the full-text index and ran at about 40 nodes per second on this data, against 1.6 thousand per second for the fallback. Both
loads give the same row counts, and the sixteen queries run on the second load (`snb-fastload`, three runs) return the same answers on
both sides with B/A between 0.70 and 1.09. Absolute times on that pass were up to 2.7 times higher than in the quiet SF0.1 table
(the machine was busy), so that table stays the reference for absolute query times:

  is the engine's node path, not the storage.

### Caveats that change how these tables should be read

- **Result shape.** IVG's `BFSFastJson` and `NKGAccel.BFSJson` return every edge reached; the Cypher queries return a
  count. The IVG count-style entry points (`KHopCount`) are not in `bench.py`. A comparison of the Q2 to Q4 rows is therefore
  not a like-for-like of the same work. Both IVG entry points are shown; none was chosen.
- **Transport.** Neo4j and Memgraph are queried over Bolt with warm query caches; IVG's ObjectScript entry points are called
  over the Native API in-process on the server, and `execute_cypher` adds translation and SQL.
- **`Q5` and `Q6`** in the traversal table are from `final-5`: a pair that is not the seed, and the weighted call with inline node ids. Neo4j rejects
  `Q5` as the repo wrote it (its variable and parameter names clash), so its cell is empty; its distinct-pair variant is in the raw results (`Q5_distinct`).
  The 100,000-node rows come from `quiet-3`, where every leg returns its full answer.
- **Memgraph algorithms.** Two images were run. The community image has no MAGE: PageRank, betweenness and SCC ran networkx
  inside the server and Louvain, Leiden, closeness and triangles are recorded as skipped. The MAGE image
  (`memgraph/memgraph-mage`) adds native PageRank, betweenness, weakly connected components, a Louvain-style detector and
  Leiden; those rows appear as `memgraph (MAGE)`. MAGE has no closeness or triangle counting; those two are Cypher compositions on the fixtures up to 2,000 nodes (rows labelled as such) and skipped above.
  Exact betweenness is not attempted above 3,000 nodes in these runs on any engine (O(nodes x edges); the 5,000 to 20,000-node section has its own runs); on the 100,000-node graph the MAGE
  and community legs run PageRank, WCC and communities only.
- **Noise of the competitor figures.** The networkx control rows (identical code on both IVG sides) differ by 20 % to 28 %
  between runs on this machine, so a competitor ratio under about 1.3 is not a ranking.
- **Published third-party numbers** (GES at SF1000 in `BENCHMARKS.md`) are quoted there as published and were not re-run.
- **LDBC.** Only the KNOWS graph exists on this machine (no datagen, no other SNB files), so only IC2, IC3, IC13 and the
  friend count could run, on the first 10,000 rows (like for like) and, for the competitors only, on all 1.9M rows (in the raw
  results). IVG's full-graph LDBC load was not run.
- **DRKG.** The repository has no DRKG query suite, so the competitor runners' `D_Q1`, `D_Q2`, `D_SP` have no IVG
  counterpart; the only DRKG comparison is loading (IVG 500,000 edges in 20 s on the alternative; Neo4j 5.87M edges in 207 s,
  Memgraph in 65 s; different sizes, the same order of edges per second only for IVG's bulk path against Neo4j's `UNWIND`).

## Not run, and why

- **LDBC SNB above SF1, and the other SNB workloads.** The interactive read queries ran at SF0.1 and SF1 on IVG (both sides), Neo4j
  and Memgraph. Not run: SF10 and above (no data was generated; the IVG sides take seconds to minutes per query at SF1 already), the
  BI workload's own query set (nine aggregates stand in for it). The interactive updates, inserts and deletes, ran at SF0.1 only. `IC13` returns no rows on
  either IVG side (on the fixed branch the natural labelled form answers; see the SNB reading) and `IC2`/`IC9` are not parity rows (the
  baseline's date comparison is wrong).
- **The engine's own edge load on SNB** was measured through a silent slow path and is corrected above: a fresh engine takes the
  per-row fallback until `initialize_schema()` has detected the deployed classes (the same on the baseline and the alternative).
  The plain `bulk_create_edges` call has a fixed cost of about 2 s per call (index disable and rebuild 1.3 s, `sync` 0.55 s, endpoint
  check 0.15 s) and 25 ms for 1,000 rows. Whether the fallback should be silent is a product question I have not touched.
- **Adding the `rdf_props` `(key, value)` index to the product.** It was tested as a scratch index in the SF1 namespaces (below) and
  helps a great deal; it is a recommendation, not a change on this branch.
- **Memgraph closeness and triangle counting**: neither Memgraph image ships a procedure; both are composed from Cypher
  (`*BFS` shortest paths; an ordered three-cycle match) up to the same node cap as the other exact algorithms, and checked against igraph
  (Pearson 1.0; 45 triangles on karate). They are Cypher compositions, not library procedures, and the rows say so.
- **Algorithms above 20,000 nodes** (exact betweenness took IVG about ten minutes at 20,000). Memgraph's sampled betweenness through `nxalg` on the
  100,000-node graph lost its server connection on the first attempt (it later ran: 253 s).
- **Repository benchmark files that do not run any work.** `tests/performance/test_ppr_stress.py` and `test_stress_v1_5.py` are marked
  expected-fail for licence exhaustion and end in a quarter of a second on both sides (tried; dropped from the matrix, a parity of nothing).
  `tests/python/test_performance_benchmarks.py` targets a container (`iris_test_vector_graph_ai`) and a REST service that do not exist here.
  `benchmarks/neo4j_comparison.py`, `tests/benchmarks/benchmark_neo4j.py` and the loaders next to them are the Neo4j side of suites that
  are covered (`NCM` in the competitor runs; `bench.py` and `bench_193_full.py` on the sides).
- **Published third-party numbers** are quoted as published, not re-run.
- Some suites report `tests-failed` on both sides because a timing gate fails under load or an Arno reader walks `USER`
  (`perf-227`'s own gate sits at 1.12 on the baseline and 1.10 on the alternative); their numbers are kept, and the status is in
  each run's files.
